# The Last Leaf

The story of Eight-Sided Ranger. Tone: gentle, witty, a little melancholy. Short sentences. No villain for its own sake. Player-facing text lives in `src/game/story.ts`; this file is the outline.

## Approved

- **Who:** the Ranger kept the Queen's woods at the very edge of Oddmere. On the morning of the Queen's wish (see Six Sided Knight) they stood on the border with one foot out. The Well was bad at edges: it gave them eight sides instead of six.
- **Why they roll on purpose:** half of them was never inside the wish, so they still choose.
- **The problem:** past the fence, the Greenwood has stopped choosing too. It has been autumn for a year and the leaves won't fall. Nobody is the villain; everyone is stuck.
- **The goal:** reach the Great Oak in the Heartwood, whose first falling leaf starts winter, and help it let go.
- **The payoff:** the Leaf face that "does nothing" is a leaf from the Oak, kept since the last good autumn. The Ranger has been holding on too.

Districts and their opening lines: see `DISTRICT_LINES` in `src/game/story.ts` (with `BEAR_LINES` and `ENDING`).

## Approved direction: the Old Bear

The Old Bear can't get to his winter sleep, because autumn won't end. He is angry about it, and he thinks it's the Ranger's fault, so he haunts them: he turns up at the end of every district (each level 10). The Ranger never harms him and tries to help. In play he can't be aimed at or snared; he moves every other turn.

## Approved: the Bear's cards

One short card the first time each level 10 is played.

1. _The Edgewood._ "Something big is crashing through the bracken. The Old Bear. He should be asleep by now, and he thinks it's your fault."
2. _Wolf Hollow._ "The Bear again. 'Every autumn the leaves fall, and I sleep,' he growls. 'Then you roll in, and nothing falls.'"
3. _The Braided River._ "He followed you across the river. Bears can swim. Bears can also hold a grudge."
4. _Antler Meadow._ "Even the stags step aside for him. Even the stags are tired."
5. _The Hush._ "At night he is quieter, and worse. He sits where you'll pass, and waits."
6. _The Heartwood._ "He is under the Great Oak. He isn't angry any more. Just very, very tired."

## Approved: the ending

After the Great Oak (reached with the Leaf face-down).

1. "There is no villain under the Great Oak. Just a tree that isn't ready for winter, and a bear who can't sleep until it is."
2. "The Ranger knows the feeling. On one of their faces is a leaf from this Oak, kept since the last good autumn."
3. "You can't make a tree let go. But you can go first. The Ranger rolls, and lets the leaf fall."
4. "The Oak lets go too. All across the Greenwood, the leaves come down."
5. "The Bear yawns. 'It wasn't you,' he says. 'It was never you.' Then he sleeps."
6. "Snow. Then spring. On purpose."

Epilogue (the Knight at the fence): "Across the fence, a six-sided Knight is watching the snow. The Ranger waves. The Knight rolls over, which is how Knights wave."
